blokken:
  - kop: The challenge
    subkop: How do you make service management recognisable and practical?
    alineas:
      - >-
        USM contains concepts and models that are needed to organise service provision well.
        The training covers services and service provision, the Agree process, the ITOCO model
        and the difference between facility, support and service, among other things.
      - But how do you convey that knowledge without starting with a theoretical explanation?
      - >-
        We turned the question around. First we show why Maria needs the knowledge. Then the
        participant works with it.
  - kop: The design question
    subkop: How do you give theory meaning through a story?
    alineas:
      - >-
        Maria's day-to-day practice is the thread running through the story. When the mayor, for
        example, has new MacBooks purchased, it soon becomes clear that arranging the product
        alone is not enough. The service around it has to be organised as well. That is exactly
        where a natural reason arises to introduce concepts from USM.
      - >-
        In this way the story answers the why behind the knowledge. That was precisely what,
        according to onITnow, was missing from the earlier set-up of separate USM MicroGames.
  - kop: The experience
    subkop: From story to practising it yourself
    alineas:
      - >-
        The participant follows Maria's story as a visual comic. At several moments the story is
        interrupted by a MicroGame in which the participant works with the knowledge themselves.
      - >-
        The original training consists of five MicroGames: four focused on core knowledge and one
        on practical examples. The full story is designed to be completed in about 120 minutes,
        at your own pace.
      - "This creates a simple rhythm:"
      - "practical situation → knowledge → practice → feedback → the story continues"
      - >-
        The theory therefore does not stand apart from practice. It gains meaning within Maria's
        search for a firmer grip on the service she provides.
  - kop: The result
    subkop: From putting out fires to a firmer grip
    alineas:
      - >-
        The MicroGame Story is a core training and introduction to USM for both new and existing
        employees. Storytelling makes clear why the knowledge matters, while the MicroGames let
        participants actively practise that knowledge.
      - >-
        Maria does not stand on her own. Within the same learning journey, stories around Pepijn
        and Jelle have also been developed. Maria focuses on services and service provision,
        Pepijn on the USM process model and Jelle on workflows.
      - >-
        In Jelle's story that development comes together: more structure helps people recognise
        recurring problems sooner, and stops the same disruptions from having to be solved over
        and over again.
  - kop: The insight
    subkop: Don't start with what someone needs to know
    alineas:
      - Maria's story shows where storytelling adds value within a MicroGame Story.
      - >-
        You can start by explaining a model or a process. You can also first create a situation
        in which the participant understands why that knowledge is needed.
      - >-
        Maria gives the theory that context. The MicroGames then let the participant work with
        it themselves.
      - First understand why the knowledge matters. Then practise with it.
