      <h2>Nine features for control after delivery</h2>
      <p>
        Prepo helps organisations use MicroGames and Stories well after delivery. For participants, this means learning
        experiences are accessible and clearly available.
      </p>
      <p>
        For organisations, this means more control over use, progress, management and further development.

      <article class="feature-item">

        <h3>Overview of projects and products</h3>
        <p>Prepo gives a clear overview of all active projects and learning products.</p>
        <p>
          You see which MicroGames, Stories or other learning experiences are available, which project they belong to
          and what status they have. That keeps the portfolio clear, even when several products run side by side.
        </p>
      </article>
      <article class="feature-item">
        <span class="feature-item__num">2</span>
        <h3>Test environment and managing testers</h3>
        <p>New versions can be tested safely first before they are shared widely.</p>
        <p>
          In the test environment, content, interaction, feedback and technology can be checked. That way you prevent
          participants from working with a version that isn’t ready for use yet.
        </p>
      </article>
      <article class="feature-item">
        <span class="feature-item__num">3</span>
        <h3>Distribution of MicroGames and Stories via public links</h3>
        <p>With Prepo, MicroGames and Stories can easily be made available to participants, teams or organisations.</p>
        <p>
          The learning experiences are offered centrally, so users know where to go and organisations keep control of
          what is being distributed.
        </p>
      </article>
      <article class="feature-item">
        <span class="feature-item__num">4</span>
        <h3>Leaderboard for players</h3>
        <p>Where appropriate, a leaderboard can be used to make progress, scores or achievements visible.</p>
        <p>
          This can motivate participants further and gives teams a playful overview of activity and results. We always
          use this deliberately: when it fits the learning goal, the audience and the desired experience.
        </p>
      </article>
      <article class="feature-item">
        <span class="feature-item__num">5</span>
        <h3>Feedback and ticketing</h3>
        <p>Feedback should not disappear into loose emails or chat messages.</p>
        <p>
          Through Prepo, feedback can be gathered in a structured way. Think of content comments, technical reports,
          improvement points or support questions. That keeps it clear what is going on and which actions are needed.
        </p>
      </article>
      <article class="feature-item">
        <span class="feature-item__num">6</span>
        <h3>Managing MicroGames, Stories and versions</h3>
        <p>Learning content changes. Processes are adjusted. Rules are renewed. Feedback leads to improvements.</p>
        <p>
          Prepo supports the management of MicroGames, Stories and versions. That keeps it clear which version is
          current, what has changed and which version participants are using.
        </p>
      </article>
      <article class="feature-item">

        <p>Prepo makes visible how learning experiences are being used.</p>

      <div class="block-head">
        <span class="section-label">Why Prepo is needed</span>
        <h2>From a one-off delivery to lasting overview</h2>
      </div>
      <p>
        Many learning products start out clear. There is a first version, a clear link and a group of participants who
        get started with it.
      </p>
      <p>But over time, fragmentation often creeps in.</p>
      <p>
        New versions arrive. Feedback is shared via email, documents or loose messages. Files get scattered. Test links
        keep circulating. Statistics are not always easy to find again. And when support is needed, it is not always
        clear which version is being used.
      </p>
      <p>Prepo prevents management from becoming a loose collection of agreements, files and links.</p>
      <p>
        It brings structure around the learning experience. That keeps it clear which products are active, which version
        is in use, where feedback comes in and how participants can reach the MicroGames or Stories.
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
