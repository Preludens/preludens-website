<section class="section">
  <div class="container">
    <div class="block-head">
      <h2>How we design learning experiences</h2>
    </div>
    <ol class="principle-list">
      <li>
        <h3>Use clear and achievable objectives.</h3>
        <p>
          Every successful design starts with a solid goal. Determine what you want to achieve, make sure it is
          achievable and establish a way to measure progress. A good design rests mainly on well-defined learning goals.
          We turn these into challenges for maximum intrinsic motivation.
        </p>
      </li>
      <li>
        <h3>Balance knowledge maximally with skills and luck to achieve challenges.</h3>
        <p>
          Gameplay must focus on expanding knowledge, and success must be achieved by applying that knowledge. Although
          skills and luck are part of gameplay, the player must strongly feel that progress is earned on the basis of
          knowledge.
        </p>
      </li>
      <li>
        <h3>Use mechanics that come from reality.</h3>
        <p>
          Knowledge arises when you can recognise and understand the context. The closer we stay to reality, the greater
          knowledge retention will remain. By connecting to mechanics people will (more or less) recognise, gameplay also
          becomes more accessible and understandable.
        </p>
      </li>
      <li>
        <h3>Focus on releasing positive, human emotions.</h3>
        <p>
          Intrinsic motivation only arises when the player is involved (through emotion) with the subject matter. This is
          not only about a need to learn, but also about a need to finish and achieve. That emotion doesn't even have to
          be linked to the learning goals to have impact.
        </p>
      </li>
      <li>
        <h3>Elements to control must be robust.</h3>
        <p>
          When players start a training, it must be robust and as bug-free as possible. Interaction with the software
          must be logical and the player must be able to get started with the object immediately, without getting lost.
          Robust also means working through the object at the right pace.
        </p>
      </li>
      <li>
        <h3>Give clear feedback, without distraction.</h3>
        <p>
          With every action (right or wrong), feedback must be given immediately about that action. There must be no
          delay. The feedback must be clear, so the player knows exactly what is expected (for now and in the future).
          Importantly, the feedback is to-the-point.
        </p>
      </li>
      <li>
        <h3>The audience must always know what the next step is.</h3>
        <p>
          The player must always know what is expected, how you can make progress and what they can do to improve that
          progress. The object must feel logical and the player must naturally get a sense of accomplishment during the
          training.
        </p>
      </li>
    </ol>
    <p>
      These principles shape how Daan and Jan-Willem design together: sharp in content, solid in technology and always
      aimed at learning that becomes applicable.
    </p>
